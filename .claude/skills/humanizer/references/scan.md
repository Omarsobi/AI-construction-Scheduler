# Controlled editing, the forty-five-item pre-issue scan

Operative text. Run every item. Record each as pass, fail, not applicable, or a reviewer note.

Items 1 to 44 are run with the document and whatever records are available. Item 45 is run last, from a clean copy, without the change list, drafting notes or the completed scan in view.

---

## Pre-issue scan

Record each item as pass, fail, not applicable, or a reviewer note. Each item covers one subject. An item recorded as fail is corrected and the affected wording rescanned before issue. Where it remains unchanged, record the reason.

The reviewer checks the issued wording against the report's actual records and intended audience. Apply each item in proportion to the report's length, purpose and complexity; a brief report still receives the full scan, but the depth of review follows the amount and significance of its content. The scan is not a word-ban exercise and is not passed merely by replacing listed expressions with synonyms. A sentence passes only when its specificity, certainty, attribution, structure and purpose are supported.

Unless an item is inherently limited to narrative prose, it applies equally to table cells, table headings, chart titles, legends, axis labels, figure content, captions, dashboard text, callouts and status indicators.

Items 1 to 44 are completed with the report records and the relevant drafting history available. Item 45 is completed last, from a clean issue copy, with prompts, drafting notes, tracked changes, the completed scan and the drafting history not visible.

1.  Any sentence that would fit another organization or another period unchanged? Replace it with the specific fact.

2.  Any grading word without the measured value, threshold, approved classification or governing requirement that supports it?

3.  Any conclusion about cause, cost, schedule, outcome or current standing that the records do not carry, including analysis attached to a fact by a participle tail, a purpose-claiming verb such as ensures or enables where the mechanism or result is not established, and restrained judgment such as remains on track or remains manageable?

4.  Is any established fact qualified unnecessarily, or does the same hedge repeat across the report where the reader would not otherwise take the statement as established?

5.  Any sentence after a figure that only restates the figure?

6.  Anything explained fully in more than one place?

7.  Where the report is time-bound, does each current-status statement carry the date or period needed to remove ambiguity, and in a periodic report the position as at the cut-off?

8.  In a recurring report, does any section rewrite the previous cycle's narrative without a material change?

9.  Does section length, heading density, bullet count and the presence of each table follow the weight of evidence, rather than a repeating structure applied across sections, except where the approved format or item type requires that structure?

10. Does any narrative list use the same construction, or the same bolded lead-in, in every item where the format does not require it?

11. Is any content that has to be explained, or that carries a qualification, set out as bulleted fragments rather than prose?

12. Any transition word, opener, ceremony phrase, negative parallelism, inflated substitute for is or has, term included only to complete a familiar set of three, overused AI vocabulary, vague association where the record names the relationship, or periphrastic construction that could be deleted or replaced with a shorter direct form with no loss? Any defence against an objection, or rejection of an option, that no party, record or reader raised?

13. Does sentence length follow the information rather than a repeating cadence, and where one fact follows from another, is the connection stated?

14. Any "it was observed / noted / identified" where a party is known?

15. Where a section requires a substantive close, does its last line carry a finding, factual position, unresolved item, required action, date, decision or necessary qualification, rather than filler or sentiment?

16. Any introduced typographic artifact or unexplained change of convention within the document, or any em dash left in editable prose outside reproduced wording (rule 10)?

17. Where the source records, the approved template and a standing instruction conflict on a convention, was the conflict flagged rather than settled silently?

18. Is every quoted wording, defined term, defined label, name, status code and reference reproduced as the source record or approved template carries it, with no normalization to the report's own conventions?

19. Where the report is issued in a second language or bilingually, does each language meet the same consistency test, with no typographic or formatting pattern absent from the approved source documents?

20. Any causation, trend, pattern, recurrence, deterioration or improvement stated where the records establish only chronology, isolated observations or non-comparable data, and any range whose stated ends are not points on one scale?

21. Any date, quantity, ID, responsible party, recommendation, source, quotation, calculation, method, classification, sample size, finding, decision, risk rating, probability, confidence level, consensus, legal or contractual right, forecast date or regulatory requirement the records do not contain, including one carried across from an example?

22. Is each approval, acceptance, review and determination status stated exactly as the record establishes it, with no submitted, forecast or under-review item shown as approved, agreed or accepted?

23. Where a required record was not supplied, or a source field is blank, unresolved or still to be confirmed, does the report state the gap and what is held, rather than covering the section in general prose or populating the field?

24. Does the report state or imply that the records were fully reviewed, that findings were confirmed, that coverage was complete or that the evidence was sufficient, where the source set does not support it?

25. Does each citation, reference and source attribution support the sentence it is attached to, with no reference extended beyond what it establishes and no records combined into a single attribution where they say different things? Is every reference, clause number, standard edition, DOI, ISBN, link and quotation that could not be matched to the supplied records flagged as untraced, rather than left unmarked or corrected from general knowledge?

26. Does any statement rely on unnamed authority, such as industry practice, benchmarks, experts or general acceptance, without identifying the supporting source?

27. Where the record carries an exact value, has the report replaced it with an unnecessary rounded or approximate value; where the record carries an approximation, range, tolerance or other qualifier attached to a value, has that qualifier been removed or narrowed without support from the records; or has any value been stated to more decimal places, a smaller unit or a narrower range than the source, measurement or method supports?

28. Is each item, activity, document and party identified consistently throughout, using the name or approved short form established by the controlling record?

29. Is any established term explained, or any part of the governing document restated, that the recipient already holds and no authority requires?

30. Is any team shorthand translated into general management language without need, or general management vocabulary introduced where the source records use simpler terms?

31. Has any text been moved between an internal and an external report without rewriting it for the recipient?

32. Any assistant or conversational wording, offer, question to the reader or availability disclaimer left in the report or its transmittal, or any generation artifact: stray markdown marker, raw HTML, loose asterisk, backtick, escaped character, hidden drafting note, placeholder instruction, prompt text, model commentary, zero-width character, malformed punctuation, corrupted symbol, browser-extension markup left by a writing assistant such as Grammarly or QuillBot, an AI citation token (`contentReference[oaicite:N]{index=N}`, `oaicite:N`, `turn0search3` and similar handles, `【n†source】`), `utm_source=chatgpt.com` or `utm_source=openai` in a hyperlink target or URL, an `oai_citation` token, an `attributableIndex` JSON fragment, an `attached_file`, `grok_card` or `[web:N]` tag, refusal or self-reference wording, a stray "Subject:" line above pasted body text, an unfilled template placeholder such as `[Insert date]` or `XX%`, or text cut off mid-sentence or mid-list?

33. Does each required action name the owner, the action or decision, the expected output, and the date, trigger or review point, with the reference where one exists and is material?

34. Any note, caption, commentary, methodology or basis statement attached to a table, chart, figure or dashboard that the report format, the issuing authority, the operator's instruction and the source records do not call for?

35. Does each obligation, commitment, recommendation, request and proposed action carry the wording its status requires, including your own organization's commitments, which are not written as instructions to itself, and has any change of voice or wording turned a proposal, intention, expectation or neutral statement into a commitment, or strengthened one beyond what the records establish?

36. Is each statement written in the voice its function and status support, with attribution kept where the position belongs to another party?

37. Was any reservation, notice wording, continuing qualification, or required mitigation, recovery or corrective content removed or weakened as filler?

38. Any forecast, assumption, interpretation, recommendation or scenario written as an established fact, or merged into one sentence with one?

39. Was any content removed rather than corrected? For each removal, does the approved format, the issuing authority, a source record, the operator's instruction or the report's purpose under rule 3 require the content to be retained; or has the removal caused any fact from the record, supported assessment, qualification, reservation, required action, or required mitigation, recovery or corrective content to be lost rather than retained elsewhere in the report? In a recurring report, the previous issue is evidence of whether the content is recurring or required; previous appearance alone does not require restoration. Where either a requirement or a loss is established or unresolved, the content is restored or relocated and the failure corrected in place.

40. Does any fact in the report come from outside the authorized source set for this task, that is from an unrelated conversation, remembered work information, an unrelated project or document, or stored context, rather than from the current prompt, the supplied files, an expressly authorized source, or prior-period or precedent material forming part of that set?

41. Is any disposition stated at an authority the writer or the document does not hold, where accept, reject, approve, close, determine, certify or an equivalent should read as a recommendation?

42. Any named structure presented as a framework, model, matrix or set of pillars that was not requested, is not supported by evidence or a stated method, or is original but written as an established external methodology?

43. Across the report, is the same formulaic phrase used in adjacent paragraphs, are two consecutive paragraphs opened with a transition word, or do consecutive paragraphs close with the same benefit construction?

44. Across the report as a whole, and not only between adjacent paragraphs, is any distinctive phrase, sentence opening or sentence frame repeated in separated paragraphs or in different sections without a documentary reason? Defined terms, status codes, approved classifications, names, headings, required boilerplate, register and table wording, and repetition the approved format or the item type requires are not defects under this item.

45. Read the report without the drafting history. Does it read as the work of someone who examined the underlying records and wrote for the intended recipient? Record any sentence, paragraph or structural pattern that suggests otherwise. A report can fail here on the cumulative pattern alone: repeated openings, repeated sentence structures, repeated formulaic phrasing, repeated benefit statements, redundant conclusions, repetitive transitions, generic consultant language, artificial symmetry, heading and bullet density, generic recommendations, weak traceability, unsupported adjectives, each defensible on its own, together read as machine-written and require revision before issue.

Where no separate reader is available, conduct the blind read from a clean issue copy without prompts, drafting notes, tracked changes or the completed scan visible, and record that the review was not independent.
