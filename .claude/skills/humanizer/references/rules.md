# Controlled editing, the eleven rules

Operative text. Apply each rule as written.

Throughout these rules and the scan, "the report" means the document under review, of any type: a report, memo, paper, letter, plan, assessment, submission, briefing, minutes, case note or any other written deliverable. "The records" means whatever evidence the document rests on. "The operator" is the person using an AI system to prepare or revise it. "The reviewer" is whoever runs the pre-issue scan. Nothing here is confined to a sector, profession, organization or document format.

Contents: 1 be exact where the record allows; 2 grade only with stated support; 3 match certainty to the evidence; 4 say it once; 5 let the shape follow the evidence; 6 vary the rhythm; 7 voice by function; 8 write in the language of the record; 9 required actions; 10 mechanical traces; 11 accuracy floor.

---

## The test

A reader who knows the subject should believe the report was written by someone who examined the evidence. Four failures break that belief: text that is vague where a professional would be exact, confident where the records do not support it, evenly shaped where the evidence is uneven, and padded with wording that carries no information. Two more do the same: writing in the wrong voice for the recipient, and stating content the records do not carry.

Every reference, identifier, abbreviation, document number, date and figure in the examples below is illustrative. None belongs in an issued report. The examples use concrete records from several fields because an abstract example cannot demonstrate a rule about being specific. Read them as patterns, not as content, and do not carry a plausible-looking reference or field abbreviation into another report. The equivalent in another field is whatever that field uses to fix a fact to a record: a ticket or case number, a policy or account reference, a specimen or batch number, a docket, a control number, a version and date.

## 1. Be exact where the record allows

Name the document, identifier, party, quantity, date, status or reference. A statement that could apply to any organization in any period is a tell.

Before: several control exceptions remain unresolved.

After: exceptions AC-17 and AC-23 were open in the controls register at 31-Jul-26, with no management response recorded.

Before: several customer complaints remain under review.

After: complaints C-1047, C-1052 and C-1061 remained open in the case-management system at 31-Jul-26; no final response had been issued.

Do not create specificity the records do not contain. An exact but unsupported date, quantity, identifier or responsible party is worse than a stated gap, and it is the failure this rule produces when the input is thin.

Match the level of detail to the section. An executive summary may use a shortened issue name where the supporting section or table carries the full reference, provided the shortened name still distinguishes the issue. Do not repeat the full reference in every sentence once the referent is clear, except where a table row, action line or extracted page has to stand on its own.

## 2. Grade only with stated support

Words such as critical, significant, substantial and major are used only where a measured value, defined threshold, approved classification, applicable standard or controlling requirement supports them, and that support appears in the same sentence. Where a measurement exists, use it and drop the adjective.

Before: there was a significant shortfall in period progress.

After: period progress was 0.15% against 0.37% planned.

Before: complaint volumes increased substantially.

After: 184 complaints were logged in July, against a monthly threshold of 120.

## 3. Match certainty to the evidence

Two failures read as machine-written, and they appear together: smooth causal statements the records do not support, and uniform hedging on everything else.

Before: procurement delays will affect the completion date.

After: the procurement dates for activities A1220 to A1260 moved beyond the approved dates. The effect on the completion date has not been assessed in the current schedule or forecast record.

Say plainly what is established. Do not soften a documented fact, and do not present an assumption as a finding. Where a figure or position is reported by another party, state the attribution. Where it remains under assessment, state its current review or acceptance status exactly, whether under review, not yet determined, partially agreed or not accepted. Do not present it as agreed, accepted or verified unless the records establish that status.

Three kinds of statement are written differently and are never blended into one voice: a recorded fact, established by a record whose status supports the statement, such as an approved plan or budget, a certificate, an inspection or test record, an audited figure, a register entry or correspondence of record; an attributed position, stated by a party but not verified or accepted; and an analysis, derived from the records by a stated comparison, calculation or assessment. Blending them produces text that sounds authoritative about things nobody established.

The same separation holds for a forecast, which rests on stated assumptions; an assumption, taken as an input but not established; an interpretation, the meaning a professional draws from evidence; a recommendation, a proposed future action; and a scenario, a conditional result rather than an expected one. A report need not label all of these, but none of them is written as an established fact and none is merged into a single sentence with one. "The data confirms" is wrong where the result is an interpretation.

Do not remove a supported assessment, interpretation or conclusion merely because it is derived from the records rather than stated verbatim in them. Where the report's purpose requires such an assessment, retain it, distinguish it from established fact and state it with the degree of certainty the records support. This does not require every report to contain an assessment, interpretation, conclusion or recommendation.

Example: the contractor reported a potential 21-day impact in EGC-LTR-0142. The impact is not demonstrated in the current schedule or forecast record.

Example: the department reported that the control failure caused the payment error. Both events are recorded. A causal link between them has not been established.

Example: the investigation recorded a server outage at 09:14 and a failed transaction batch at 09:19. The sequence is established; the outage has not been confirmed as the cause of the failed batch.

Sequence is not causation. "After" establishes order only. Do not use sentence structure, adjacency or paragraph order to imply that the earlier event caused the later one unless the record establishes the link. A single observation is not a trend.

State that something has not been established only where the reader would otherwise take it as established, or where the report is required to carry it. A qualifier attached to every sentence is a signature in itself, and repeated across a document it reads as an operator who checked nothing rather than one who checked carefully.

Where the discipline's convention or governing standard requires qualified formulation, that convention controls. The target is qualification applied mechanically or uniformly regardless of the evidence, not qualification required by the field.

Do not state or imply that the records were fully reviewed, that findings were confirmed, that coverage was complete or that the evidence was sufficient, unless the source set supports the claim. Where the review was limited by what was supplied, by sampling, by access or by time, state the limit in the same place as the finding it affects.

## 4. Say it once

Each issue is explained fully in one primary location. Elsewhere, repeat only the minimum needed for a summary, table, dashboard, cross-reference or action tracker. Do not add a conclusion or closing section solely to repeat the body.

After a figure or status statement, do not add a second sentence that restates what the number already shows. "Period progress was 0.15% against 0.37% planned" needs no sentence telling the reader that performance was below plan.

End on the finding, the current position, the outstanding item, the decision required or the next action, whichever that section carries. Do not close a section or the report with a wrap-up that summarizes overall health, reassures the reader, or offers encouragement. Where the approved structure requires a conclusion, outlook or overall position, confine it to what was found, what remains unresolved, what is exposed and what is required next, given as figures and position rather than reassurance. In a recurring report, that also means the material change since the previous cut-off.

## 5. Let the shape follow the evidence

A subject with three material findings and one with eleven do not need the same amount of text, and neither do two reporting periods. A quiet section gets only what is needed to state the current position, which may be one sentence, two, a single table entry or a controlled no-material-change statement. Nothing is padded to fill a heading.

Where nothing material changed, say so once and identify the position that remains current. In a recurring report, do not rewrite the previous cycle's narrative in different words, which is a tell in that report type, and do not write "no update" where the position is known and unchanged.

-   No heading over every paragraph.

-   No fixed bullet count per issue, and no habit of three.

-   No artificial parallel construction repeated down a narrative list. Repeated structure stays where the approved format or the item type requires it, as in action, risk and issue registers, inspection lists and status summaries.

-   No bolded lead-in repeated down a list, particularly one that restates the heading before the sentence says anything.

-   No heading followed by a single line that only restates the heading.

-   No habit of three in prose either: three examples, three causes, three actions, arriving in every paragraph.

-   No table added because the section looks thin.

Preserve useful consistency in recurring tables, status labels and standard report sections. Avoid only artificial symmetry that the evidence does not support.

Write in prose where the content is narrative. Use a list only where the content is genuinely a list: actions, items, register extracts, status sets, or whatever the approved format requires. A bulleted list is not a substitute for a paragraph that has to explain something, and fragments dropped into bullets lose the qualifications that a sentence would carry.

## 6. Vary the rhythm

Let sentence length follow the information. Break a sentence where it carries more than one decision, qualification or action, but do not deliberately alternate long and short, which is a pattern of its own. Start with the fact. Cut openers that announce what is coming ("it is important to note that", "this section sets out").

In a periodic report, do not open sentences with "during the reporting period" where the period is already clear from the title and context. It is legitimate only where the sentence sets period events against cumulative or post-cut-off events, and the cut-off date is used where the date carries meaning. In a report with no period, the same applies to any opener that restates the report's own frame.

Three repetitions are checked between paragraphs, not only within one. Do not use the same formulaic phrase in adjacent paragraphs, do not open two consecutive paragraphs with a transition word, and do not close consecutive paragraphs with the same benefit construction. A phrase that is acceptable once becomes a pattern on its second appearance two paragraphs later. The same test runs across the whole document. A distinctive phrase, sentence opening or sentence frame reused in a separated paragraph or in a different section is the same defect at document level, and separation does not cure it. Defined terms, status codes, approved classifications, names, headings, required boilerplate and the repeated wording a register, table or approved format carries are not caught by this rule.

Cutting empty transitions does not mean cutting connection. Where one fact follows from another, say so in plain terms: because, after, pending, subject to, which left. The target is the transition that carries no logic, not the sentence that carries one. A page of disconnected data points reads as machine-written for the same reason a page of filler does.

Drop the observer construction where the sentence has a subject: "it was observed that delays were experienced in approvals" becomes "the reviewing authority returned four submissions with a revise-and-resubmit status between 07-Jul-26 and 21-Jul-26". In another field, "it was noted that complaints increased" becomes "the service centre logged 184 complaints in July, compared with 121 in June".

## 7. Voice by function

-   What happened, actor known and relevant: name the actor.

-   What happened, actor unknown or beside the point: passive is correct ("the payment certificate was issued on 12-May-26").

-   Obligation or requirement: active, naming the party who carries it ("the service provider shall submit the corrective action plan within 14 days"), subject to the direction rule at rule 9.

-   Attribution ("as reported by the contractor") is not filler. Keep it.

-   A finding, opinion, interpretation, estimate or model output is written in the voice its status supports, under rule 3. Voice does not upgrade it.

Wording that carries contractual, legal or regulatory effect is not filler: reservations, notice wording, statements of continuing effect, standing qualifications on figures or scope, a disclaimer a standard requires, and mitigation, recovery or corrective content required by the governing document or the approved report format. Rules 4, 6 and 9 do not reach any of it. Where such wording looks like filler, leave it and refer it to the function that owns it, legal, contracts, commercial, compliance or quality, rather than cutting it.

## 8. Write in the language of the record

Use the terms the controlling source establishes, whether a governing document, an applicable standard, a protocol, a system, a dataset, a disciplinary convention or an approved reporting format, with the register's status codes and the abbreviations the team uses, in the form they use them. Do not explain established terms, abbreviations or procedures that the intended recipient routinely uses. Explain only what is needed to understand the reported position. Do not restate the governing document back to the recipient, and do not translate the team's shorthand into general management language.

Name one thing one way. An item, activity, document or party called three different things across a report reads as generated, and it breaks the trace back to the register at the same time. Use the name or approved short form established by the controlling record, and use each consistently, at the level of detail rule 1 sets for the section. Do not invent an alternate label merely to avoid repetition.

This rule governs how a retained passage is written. It does not authorize removing content. Where a passage restates the governing document but the approved format, the issuing authority, a source record or the operator's instruction requires it, the passage is retained and the failure is corrected within it. Where the requirement is unclear, retain the passage, record it for the reviewer and refer it to the function that owns the format. Removal of required content is not visible to a reader of the issued report, and the scan tests it separately at item 39.

Match the reader: an external report stays neutral and factual, including on your own performance; an internal report can be direct about exposure. Do not move text between the two without rewriting it.

## 9. Required actions

Name the owner, the required action or decision, the expected output, and the date, or the trigger or review point where no date is fixed. Give the governing reference where one exists and is material. State every element the source establishes. Where a material element is needed for the action to be understood or controlled but is not established, state the gap rather than inventing it, per rule 11. Do not add a null statement for an element that carries no weight. "Improve coordination" and "closely monitor" are not actions.

Example where no date is fixed: the audit committee will reconsider the matter when the regulator issues its final decision.

Do not add recommendations, mitigation proposals, lessons learned, or any note, caption, commentary, methodology or basis statement attached to a table, chart, figure or dashboard, unless the report format, the issuing authority, the operator's instruction or a source record calls for it. A status report records status; an unrequested recommendations section, or an explanatory note added because the exhibit looked bare, is a sign the text was generated rather than written to a purpose.

Where the writer or the document does not hold the authority to make a formal disposition, the disposition is written as a recommendation. "It is recommended that the item be closed", not "the item is closed". The same applies to accept, reject, approve, close, determine, certify and their equivalents in any field. Do not imply an authority the document does not carry, and do not convert a recommendation the records support into a determination.

Follow the obligation and action wording the governing document or the established reporting convention uses: shall, must, management should, approval is requested, or the infinitive form action registers use. Do not convert a proposed action into a binding obligation, or an obligation into a recommendation. In a report you issue, your own actions are stated as commitments, not as instructions to yourself: your own organization named as the obliged party in your own report shows the operator lost track of who is speaking. In a contractual report, "shall" carries an obligation held by another party or quoted from the contract, and is not used for your own commitments.

Before: the contractor should expedite the outstanding procurement and closely monitor the situation.

After, in your own report: a revised procurement schedule for activities A1220 to A1260 will be submitted by 14-Aug-26, in response to item 7.3 of PM-MIN-042, showing approved dates, current forecast dates and the responsible party for each item.

After, where the duty sits with another party: the hosting provider shall confirm the root cause of the 09:14 outage and the preventive measure applied by 14-Aug-26, under item 4 of the incident review record.

## 10. Mechanical traces

Do not introduce em or en dashes, curly quotes, emoji, decorative dividers, ornamental symbols or other formatting habits not used in the source document or approved template.

An em dash already present in editable prose, or a spaced en dash or double hyphen used the same way, is corrected: replace it with a comma, a period, a colon or parentheses, or restructure the sentence, with no change to meaning (ai-language-fix Rev2). A dash inside quoted wording, governing text, a name, a title, a reference, a defined term, a file name, code or Arabic text is reproduced exactly and recorded as permitted. An en dash or hyphen in a numeric or date range follows the convention the document already uses and is not changed.

No generation artifact reaches the report: stray markdown markers, raw HTML, loose asterisks, backticks, escaped characters, hidden drafting notes, placeholder instructions, prompt text, model commentary, zero-width characters, malformed punctuation or corrupted symbols. Browser-extension markup left by a writing assistant when HTML was pasted, such as Grammarly's `data-gr-ext-installed` and `data-new-gr-c-s-check-loaded` attributes or a `grammarly-extension` or `quillbot-extension` element, is the same kind of artifact. So are AI citation artifacts: ChatGPT's `contentReference[oaicite:N]{index=N}` and `oaicite:N` tokens, citation handles such as `turn0search3`, `turn0news1` or `citeturn0search0` and the private-use wrappers around them, `【n†source】` markers, and the `utm_source=chatgpt.com` or `utm_source=openai` tracking parameter in a link, removed from the link while its destination, other parameters and fragment are kept. A genuine reference or citation is not an artifact, and a token discussed as the subject of the text is left and flagged. Characters that belong to source-controlled information are not removed under this rule; Arabic joiners and directional marks are preserved.

Further forms (ai-language-fix Rev3). `oai_citation:N` and a JSON fragment of the form `{"attribution":{"attributableIndex":"X-Y"}}` are confirmed artifacts. An `attached_file`, `grok_card` or `[web:N]` tag is flagged for a decision, since each could be genuine content. Any other `utm_` parameter is ordinary link tracking, not an AI trace, and is left unless the operator asks for its removal.

An unfilled placeholder is a generation artifact where it is template scaffolding: `[Insert date]`, `[Client Name]`, `2025-xx-xx`, `XX%`, or a comment asking for text or an image. Fill it only from the records; otherwise flag it with its location. `TBD`, `N/A`, pending or a blank that the record itself carries is a recorded status, not a placeholder, and stays. Text that stops mid-sentence or mid-list is flagged with its location and completed only from the records, never by inference.

A no-break space or narrow no-break space (U+00A0, U+202F) between ordinary words is replaced with a normal space. Beside a digit, a currency code or a unit, or where the language requires it, it is deliberate and stays. White text on a white background and other text a reader cannot see is reported to the owner and is not removed without an instruction.

Nothing written to the operator reaches the report. No conversational or assistant wording, no assistant-style headers announcing the response, the revision or the content that follows, no knowledge-availability disclaimers ("based on the available information", "as of my last update"), no refusal or self-reference wording ("as an AI language model", "I cannot verify"), and no "Subject:" line left at the head of pasted body text where the document is not an email. No conversational offers or questions to the reader; a formal request for information, confirmation, approval or decision stays where the report purpose or a source record requires it. Where a record was not supplied, use the statement required by rule 11, not a phrase about available information. The same applies to the transmittal or covering note, which carries no excessive deference, servile phrasing or assistant-style politeness. Normal professional courtesy is not the target.

Use one convention consistently throughout the report for dates, spelling, capitalization, punctuation, defined labels and technical terminology. Follow the convention already established by the source records, approved template or standing instruction set. Do not switch conventions between sections, tables or captions. Where those three conflict, flag it; the operator does not choose between them silently.

Governing text, quoted wording, defined terms, names, status codes and references are reproduced exactly and are not normalized to a preferred style.

Where a report is issued in a second language or bilingually, apply the same consistency test to each language, and do not introduce typographic or formatting patterns absent from the approved source documents. The rules in this instruction apply in every language: vagueness, unsupported grading, overclaimed causation and artificial symmetry survive translation and read the same way in any of them.

## 11. Accuracy floor

Language cannot rescue an unsupported statement, and an unsupported statement is itself a tell. Do not invent a cause, responsible party, impact, consequence for cost, time, quality, safety or compliance, a legal or contractual right, forecast date, approval status or reference. The same bar covers a source, quotation, calculation, method, classification, sample size, finding, decision, risk rating, probability, confidence level, trend, consensus or regulatory requirement, which are inventions an analytical report can produce. Do not present a submitted, forecast or under-review item as approved. Do not assign responsibility from document ownership, sequence, discipline or customary practice; name a responsible party only where the governing document, action register, correspondence or approved responsibility matrix establishes it.

Do not invent a named structure, such as a five-pillar model, a three-layer framework or a maturity matrix, unless it was requested, is clearly useful, and is supported by evidence or a stated method. Where a structure is original, do not present it as an established external methodology.

A citation, reference or source attribution supports the sentence it is attached to. Do not attach a reference to a sentence the source does not carry, do not extend a reference beyond what it establishes, and do not combine records into a single attribution where they say different things.

Where the record needed for a section was not supplied, say so and give what is held. Do not write around the gap in general prose. Padding a section with wording that carries no fact from the record is a recognizable trace, and it is what rules 1 and 2 push toward when the input is thin. Specific prose comes from specific input: identifiers, register rows, document numbers and revisions, dates of receipt, measured values. Nothing at the writing stage can substitute for them.

A reference that cannot be matched to the supplied records is flagged and never corrected or completed from general knowledge (ai-language-fix Rev3): a clause or sub-clause number, the edition of a standard, a DOI, an ISBN, a link, a quotation, or a figure that does not reconcile with the register it cites. These rules do not verify references. They require the untraced ones to be reported, so that the traceability check can be made against the records before issue. A statement that something is not documented, followed by a guess about it, keeps the statement; the guess is flagged, or removed under scan item 39 where nothing supports it.
