## Annex: machine-generated wording and sentence patterns to challenge

Strike or replace the following unless the sentence passes the tests in rules 1 to 3, or the wording is protected text reproduced from a source.

The entries are illustrative, not a closed list. Several are drawn from one field; the equivalent habit in another field is challenged the same way.

Rebuild the sentence around the fact, or delete it. Never swap a listed word for a synonym: "significant" becoming "notable", "closely monitor" becoming "keep under review", "several activities" becoming "a number of activities" leaves the same absence of fact behind a word the checker no longer catches. Where no fact supports the sentence, remove it and state the gap under rule 11.

A listed word is not challenged where it is a defined term, approved classification or term of art used in its established technical meaning. The supporting definition, threshold or context is retained where needed.

**Sentence patterns, not just words:** participle tails that manufacture analysis ("the reviewing authority returned four submissions, highlighting the need for improved coordination"); negative parallelism ("not only was the submission returned, it was returned for revision", "this is not a data issue, it is a process issue"); inflated substitutes for "is" or "has" (serves as, represents, marks, features); repeated hyphenated modifier habits (cross-functional, data-driven, real-time), particularly where the source records use simpler terms of their own; and one referent named three ways in one report. Rule of three in noun lists (cost, time and quality; people, process and technology; scope, schedule and budget) where a term carries no fact and appears only to complete a familiar set. Purpose-claiming verbs (ensures, enables, allows, serves to, plays a key role in), kept only where the records establish the mechanism or result claimed.

**Arguing with no one:** to be clear, this is not to say, this is not to suggest, this should not be read as, I am not saying, do not get me wrong, one might argue, a tempting approach would be, an obvious alternative would be, and any option set out only to be rejected. The text answers an objection or dismisses an option that no party, record or reader raised, usually a leftover from an earlier draft. Remove the defence; where it holds a real claim, state the claim. Keep a reply to a position a party actually took, in a letter, comment, claim or minute, with that position attributed; keep an option the reader would actually weigh, with the reason the record gives. Reservation wording such as "without prejudice to" is protected wording, not this pattern.

**Vague association:** associated with, in association with, linked to, tied to, connected to, where the record names the actual relationship: issued by, caused by, part of, instructed under, submitted against. Name the relationship the record gives. Where the record does not say, keep the vague wording rather than invent one. "In connection with" used by the formal correspondence register to point at a cited letter is register, not this pattern.

**Overused AI vocabulary:** underscore (verb), showcase, highlight (verb), landscape (abstract), testament, tapestry, garner, interplay, intricate, meticulous, vibrant, enduring, bolstered, delve, commendable, realm, multifaceted, groundbreaking, notably. These appear in machine-generated text far more often than in professional writing, most of all in clusters. Rebuild the sentence around the fact under the rule above; a synonym swap leaves the same gap. A word used in its technical meaning, or inside a quotation, title or defined term, is not challenged.

**Assistant residue:** I hope this helps, let me know if you would like, here is the updated section, as of my last update, based on the available information, would you like me to expand, please find below, as an AI language model, certainly, great question, you are absolutely right, and an email "Subject:" line left above pasted body text. Citation and link residue from a chat interface is the same kind of trace: `:contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}`, `[oaicite:1]`, `turn0search3`, `turn0click1`, `turn0fetch2`, `turn0view1`, `turn0news1`, `citeturn0search0`, `【4†source】` `oai_citation:1`, an `{"attribution":{"attributableIndex":"0-12"}}` fragment, and the `utm_source=chatgpt.com` or `utm_source=openai` link parameter. `attached_file`, `grok_card` and `[web:1]` tags are flagged for a decision. Remove the token and the parameter, not the reference or link it sits beside.

**Certainty and qualification:** hedge stacking, may potentially, could possibly, appears to suggest, may perhaps indicate. Use the degree of qualification the evidence supports or the discipline requires, not several overlapping hedges.

**Grading without support:** critical concern, significant milestone, robust strategy, comprehensive assessment, proactive approach, seamless coordination, noteworthy achievement, substantial improvement, strategic alignment, pivotal role, key success factor, best-in-class, transformational, major breakthrough. The same test applies to crucial, effective, efficient, holistic, dynamic, transformative and optimized. Where a measured value, defined threshold, approved classification or governing requirement supports the word, that support appears in the same sentence; where a measurement exists, use it and drop the adjective.

**"Ensure" and "key":** "ensure" is used only where the stated action can make the outcome certain. Where it only improves the likelihood, the accurate verb is support, reduce the risk of, improve, confirm, verify, maintain, establish, demonstrate or provide. "Key" is used only where genuine prioritization is intended and the report establishes the ranking: key issue, key risk, key finding, key consideration, key stakeholder are challenged where nothing distinguishes the item from the others. Neither word is a defect where it is a defined term, an approved classification or a fixed heading in the controlling format.

**Generic consultant language:** drive value creation, unlock opportunities, enhance operational efficiency, foster collaboration, strengthen governance, optimize outcomes, support strategic objectives, improve stakeholder alignment, implement robust processes, establish a comprehensive framework. These carry no numerical allowance and no accuracy exception. Retain one only where it communicates a specific supportable meaning; otherwise replace it with the action, the responsible party, the required output, the timing, the evidence, the decision and the measurable or practical result.

**Generic benefit constructions:** this ensures, this enables, this supports, this helps, this demonstrates, this allows, this facilitates, this improves. Challenged wherever they occur, mid-paragraph as much as at a paragraph ending. A benefit sentence added to round off a paragraph is removed; a benefit that is relevant, specific and supported by the records is kept and written as a statement of what the record shows. Do not use the same construction in adjacent paragraphs.

**The obvious as a finding:** delays can affect completion; cost increases affect budget; communication improves coordination; risks should be managed; monitoring improves visibility. A general truth restated as an analytical finding tells the reader nothing about the matter under review. Remove it unless it serves a required documentary purpose.

**Rhetorical balancing:** not only X but also Y; rather than X, Y; not merely X, but Y; while X, Y; on the one hand, on the other hand; and the clipped negative tail, "the options come from the selected item, no guessing". Kept only where the contrast materially changes the meaning, and not repeated as a habit of phrasing.

**Vague where specifics exist:** several activities, various issues, some delays, relevant stakeholders, necessary actions, appropriate measures, certain documents, a number of concerns, in due course, as soon as possible.

**Vague authority:** industry practice indicates, benchmarks suggest, experts consider, observers have noted, it is generally accepted, best practice requires, where no named or cited authority supports the statement.

**Unnecessary approximation:** approximately, around, roughly or about used where the record carries an exact value.

**Artificial completeness:** every field in a table, tracker or register is populated even though the source records contain blanks, unresolved values, not-yet-received items or information to be confirmed. A cell may legitimately read blank, N/A, not applicable, pending, not confirmed, not available, disputed or to be verified, and the correct entry is whichever of these the record supports. The same applies to padding for shape: an exactly three, five or seven item list, an equal count of problems and solutions or of risks and mitigations, matched paragraph or bullet lengths, and parallel section structures the evidence does not produce. Report the number of items the evidence supports.

**False precision:** a value is stated to more decimal places, a smaller unit or a narrower range than the source, measurement or method supports.

**Periphrasis:** in order to, due to the fact that, at this point in time, has the ability to, is in the process of. Hidden verbs, where the plain verb says the same thing and no defined term is lost: carry out an analysis of (analyze), make a determination (determine), give consideration to (consider), undertake a review of (review). "There is" or "there are" opening a sentence that has a real subject.

**Ceremony before an ordinary point:** at its core, the real question is, fundamentally, what really matters, the deeper issue, in today's fast-paced environment, a game-changer, navigate the complexities, a step in the right direction, whether you are X or Y.

**False ranges:** constructions such as "issues ranged from procurement delays to stakeholder alignment" where the stated ends are not points on one scale.

**Carries no information:** the project continues to make steady progress; the team remains committed to the project objectives; several challenges were encountered; stakeholders should continue to monitor the situation; appropriate mitigation measures should be implemented; continued collaboration will be essential; the contractor is taking proactive steps; the issue requires close attention. Reflexive balance, such as while the shortfall is recorded, it should be balanced against the wider progress achieved, where the records establish the shortfall but not the balancing position.

**Openers, transitions and closings:** furthermore, moreover, additionally, in conclusion, it is important to note that, it should be highlighted that, from a broader perspective, in this regard, going forward, with that being said, and "during the reporting period" used as a routine opener where the period is already clear. Restatement openers, in summary, overall, taken together, on balance, where they introduce wording that repeats the section rather than an overall finding the report requires.

**Unsupported management judgment:** remains on track, remains manageable, recovery is achievable, management attention is required, the position is under control, the risk is being effectively managed, no immediate concern is anticipated, the trend warrants intervention, the current approach remains appropriate. These read as restrained rather than inflated, and they can survive an edit that removes the obvious filler.

**Non-actions:** improve coordination, closely monitor the issue, implement appropriate mitigation, expedite the works, take corrective action, enhance communication.
