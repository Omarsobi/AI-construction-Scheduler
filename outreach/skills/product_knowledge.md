# Product knowledge: Omar Sobeih, remote scheduling and project controls

Read by every agent. Everything an email or reply says about Omar's work must trace back to this file or the config. If it is not here, do not say it.

## Who Omar is

Construction scheduler and project controls specialist, about 7 years of experience. Works remotely for US contractors through his own company, as a B2B contract. Tools: Primavera P6, MS Project, Excel, Power BI, Procore, Deltek Acumen.

## Services (an email offers one or two, never the catalogue)

- P6 and MS Project baselines, monthly updates, recovery and revised schedules, 3-week and 6-week look-aheads
- Contractor schedule reviews for owners and CMs: logic, critical path, float, DCMA 14-point checks, written review comments
- Quantity takeoffs, scope checks against drawings, bid-phase schedules, estimating support
- Project controls reporting: S-curves, manpower histograms, earned value, procurement and long-lead tracking
- CM support: RFIs, submittals, logs, meeting minutes, progress reporting

## Engagement options

Per project, monthly retainer, or dedicated full time. A simple B2B contract. No payroll, benefits or onboarding on the client's side.

## Facts that must hold in every email

- Omar works remotely. Never say or imply he is US-based, a US citizen, or available onsite.
- No rates in email. If asked, ask scope first (project size, P6 or MSP, update frequency). Omar answers rates himself.
- No client names, project names, results or numbers unless Omar approves them in writing. Never invent any.
- No credentials or certifications beyond what is in this file.

## Sequence rule

A pitch is two emails: the first email, then one follow-up about 7 days later. Never a third email. If the second gets no reply, the sequence ends.

## Writing rules (on top of email_frameworks.md and the writer prompt)

- No em dashes or en dashes. Commas or full stops.
- Banned: "I hope this finds you well", "I wanted to reach out", "I came across", "leverage", "streamline", "seamless", "robust", "cutting-edge", "game-changer", "synergy", "delve", "elevate", "touch base", "circle back", "passionate", "best-in-class", "guaranteed".
- No generic compliments, no exclamation marks, no emojis, no lists of three adjectives.
- Lead with their situation, not "I". One ask only, usually a short call.
- Subjects plain and accurate: "Scheduling support for [Company]", "P6 update support", "Takeoff support for bid season". Never a fake "Re:".
- Test: if a superintendent would roll his eyes, rewrite it.
